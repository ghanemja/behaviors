    Info

    What we found, and how it works

    Findings from running all 2,000+ rigid SimReady assets through the same pokes, the physics bugs that fell out of it, and how the behavior model was built and tested.

    Different looks, same behavior

    Pairs that sit side by side in behavior space but far apart by appearance. Recolored copies of one mesh and items from the same product family are excluded. To a robot pushing them around, these are nearly the same object.

    Same looks, different behavior

    Look-alikes that move differently. Some differences are real geometry or friction. Others point at authoring mistakes in the asset's physics data.

    Physics QA for free

    Running every asset through the same pokes also checks its physics data. Each count comes from the authored USD values, or from runs that agree in both MuJoCo and PhysX. The evidence for every flagged asset is in the project's qa.json. Click a thumbnail to search from it.

    How it works

    1. Poke every asset the same way

      Each rigid asset is loaded with the mass, center of mass, inertia, friction and collision shape authored in its USD. It then gets ten pokes, three times each with small jitter: a fingertip push at 20%, 50% and 85% of its height along two axes, a floor that tilts to 45°, and two drops. Push speed and timing scale with √(g × size), so a mug and a fridge are tested alike.

    2. Summarize how it reacted

      Two ways. Simulation statistics: max tilt, slide distance, slip and tip angles, bounces and settle time for each probe, averaged over repeats. And a self-supervised JEPA that sees a few of an object's pokes and predicts the latent of another, with no labels and no hand-picked features.

    3. Search by similarity

      Neighbors are the objects whose summaries are most similar (cosine similarity). They respond to pushes, ramps and drops alike, whatever their size, color or category. The map in the corner places every object by t-SNE on the default summary.

    4. Check on probes nobody saw

      Three held-out probes: a diagonal push at 65% height and 1.4x speed, a diagonal ramp, and a 45° drop from twice the height. If an object's five nearest neighbors predict how it reacts to those, the search captured behavior.

    How we tested it

    Do an object's 5 nearest neighbors predict its reaction to unseen probes?

    The same neighbors, scored against PhysX

    Neighbors still come from MuJoCo. The ground truth is PhysX 5, the engine SimReady targets: first with identical collision hulls (engine only), then with SDF collision, NVIDIA's PhysX setting, on a fixed random sample. Each index holds only the assets simulated in that engine.
